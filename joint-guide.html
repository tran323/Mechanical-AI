Three things every joint depends on

Gap words: press, snug, sliding, free

Layers are the weak direction

Your 1.0 mm nozzle changes the sizes

Most numbers here come from guides written for a 0.4 mm nozzle. Your printer lays 1.1 mm wide lines and 0.4 mm layers, so:

  • The thinnest wall or rib is 2.2 mm (2 lines); a single 1.1 mm line works only for fins and thin springs. Snap arms and screw bosses come out thicker than the guides say.
  • Draw walls in steps of 1.1 mm: 2.2, 3.3, 4.4.
  • Draw heights in steps of 0.4 mm. A gap in Z needs at least 0.4 mm, and more than one layer if it must stay open.
  • Holes print small, and a fat nozzle makes small holes even smaller.
  • The blue Your Ender column in each table is my first guess. Print a fit-test coupon (pins and holes in 0.05 mm steps) before you trust it.

Family P

Printed plastic joints

No screws and no metal: the shape of the plastic does the work. These are the joints most printed parts use.

P1

Snap-fit

A springy arm with a hook. Push the parts together: the arm bends, then the hook clicks behind a ledge.

  • Comes apartOnly with a 45° back face
  • Extra partsNone
  • Holding powerLight
  • Best plasticPETG

✓Use it for

  • Lids, covers and battery doors
  • Clips that need no tools
  • Fast assembly of many parts

✕Skip it when

  • The part is PLA and stays bent for months (PLA creeps and lets go)
  • The arm would have to stand up in Z
  • It must carry a real load

P4

P5

Printed thread

A spiral ridge on the neck screws into a spiral groove in the cap. Big and coarse works; small and fine doesn’t.

  • Comes apartYes, many times
  • Extra partsNone
  • Holding powerMedium
  • Best plasticPETG or PLA

✓Use it for

  • Jar lids and screw caps
  • Big knobs, and printed nuts on printed bolts
  • Containers you open often

✕Skip it when

  • The thread is smaller than about M6 (use a heat-set insert, H2)

P6

Pin & hole

A peg on one part fits into a hole in the other. It lines parts up. On its own it holds very little pull.

  • Comes apartYes; glue it to make it permanent
  • Extra partsNone
  • Holding powerAligns, weak pull
  • Best plasticPETG or PLA
Key fact

A tight round press-fit does not work on FDM. The allowed squeeze is smaller than the printer’s error. Use a shape that wedges, flexes or gets glued.

✓Use it for

  • Lining up two printed halves before screws or glue
  • Stopping parts sliding sideways
  • Parts glued together (the pin sets the position)

✕Skip it when

  • Friction alone must hold the parts together
  • A tall, thin pin stands up in Z (it shears at a layer line)

Screws made for plastic (ST sizes) use a pilot of about 0.8 × size: ST2.2 → 1.8, ST2.9 → 2.3, ST3.5 → 2.8, with a boss of 5.0, 6.5 and 8.0. Machine-screw pilots on a 1.0 mm nozzle are a guess: print a small ladder of holes and try them.

When it goes wrong

What goes wrongFix
Boss splitsWalls too thin or pilot too small: 2 full lines of wall, slightly bigger pilot

Boss snaps offAdd gussets and a fillet at the base

How to print it: stand the boss up (screw axis in Z) so the screw pulls along the layers.

H2

Heat-set insert

A small brass sleeve with a thread inside. A hot soldering iron melts it into the hole, so you get a metal thread in plastic.

  • Comes apartMany times
  • Extra partsInsert, screw, soldering iron
  • Holding powerStrong
  • Best plasticPETG or PLA

✓Use it for

  • Parts you open often
  • Electronics cases and lids
  • A firm metal thread where a nut won’t fit

✕Skip it when

  • The wall can’t be at least 2 lines thick
  • You have no soldering iron
  • The hole runs sideways (hard to press in straight)

H3

Nut + bolt

A metal bolt goes through both parts into a metal nut. Metal threads do all the work, so it is the strongest joint here.

  • Comes apartMany times
  • Extra partsBolt and nut
  • Holding powerStrongest
  • Best plasticAny (PETG for clamps)

✓Use it for

  • Brackets, frames and mounts
  • Anything pulled, shaken or loaded
  • Joints you take apart again and again

✕Skip it when

  • There is no room for the nut or the tool
  • You want a clean outside with no nut showing (use H2)

H4

Bearing + shaft

A steel ball bearing sits in a printed pocket and a steel rod goes through its middle. It turns smoothly for a long time.

  • Comes apartYes, and it turns
  • Extra partsBearing, steel rod
  • MotionTurns under load
  • Best plasticPETG or nylon pocket

✓Use it for

  • Wheels, pulleys and rollers
  • Knobs and arms that turn a lot or under load
  • Anything that must turn smoothly for years

✕Skip it when

  • It turns slowly, lightly and rarely: a printed bushing on a steel pin is enough
  • There is no room for a bearing
